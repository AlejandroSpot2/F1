CHECKERED FLAG / VERDICT

Different? Yes. Rules did it? Not proven.

In this matched sample, 2026 was usually slower and the running order usually moved less. Any-caution laps rose slightly, while fully neutralized laps fell slightly. The era looks different. Calling the regulations the cause—or the racing better—would outrun the data.

PARC FERMÉ / METHOD

How the lap was timed.

Sample and provenance

Race-session data comes from FastF1 3.8.1. The current bundle contains 18 races at 9 matched circuits through Great Britain, generated for this release. The official 2026 calendar is still in progress, so this is a snapshot rather than a whole-season verdict.

Pace definitions

Median pace is the median of each driver's best accurate green-flag lap. Speed trap is the median of each driver's maximum speed-trap reading on an accurate green lap. Both reduce one-off noise, but neither controls weather, tires or track evolution.

Running-order movement

For each driver, absolute position changes are summed only across consecutive observed laps, then normalized per 100 driver-lap transitions. Pit cycles, retirements and Safety Cars count; this is deliberately labelled movement, never overtakes.

Race-control states

Caution-affected laps include local yellow, VSC, Safety Car and red flag. Full neutralization excludes local-yellow-only laps. Each state is sampled on the P1 car's lap clock, preventing lapped cars' asynchronous personal lap numbers from smearing one incident across adjacent race laps.

Limits and sources
  • Descriptive same-circuit comparison; no causal identification.
  • Weather, tire compound, track evolution, strategy and field composition are not controlled.
  • FastF1 documentation
  • Official 2026 race results
