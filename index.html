Race Comparison Overview

This project compares Australia, China, Japan, Miami, and Canada across 2025 and 2026 using FastF1 race data, with the emphasis on pace, top speed, running-order volatility, and race disruption.

Lap 01

First, compare raw pace.

Lap time and speed-trap numbers are the cleanest way to see whether a circuit got faster, slower, or simply different from one season to the next.

Circuit Comparison Bars

These bars compare lap-time and straight-line speed measures by race. Lower lap times mean faster laps, while higher speed-trap values mean more speed at the end of a straight.

Boost Pad

Then watch the running order.

F1 race position is not just who is fastest. Pit stops, tire life, Safety Cars, and traffic can all reshuffle the order lap by lap.

Lap-by-Lap Position Step Charts

Each line follows one leading driver through the race. Position 1 is first place, so lines near the top show drivers running closer to the lead.

Overtake Zone

Now summarize the chaos.

The proxy chart compresses movement into one score: higher values mean the race order changed more often, even if the cause was strategy instead of a clean pass.

Position-Change Proxy

This compares how much the race order moved around at each circuit. Higher values suggest more passing, pit-stop reshuffling, incidents, or recovery drives.

Caution Lap

Race control changes the story.

A Safety Car or red flag can erase gaps and reset strategy. The timeline shows where those neutralized laps happened.

Incident and Neutralization Timeline

Each row is one race and each block is one lap. Green means normal racing; yellow, Safety Car, VSC, and red flag blocks mark caution or stoppage periods.

Final Lap

Finish with the scorecard.

The last chart puts every circuit and metric in one grid so the 2025 to 2026 changes are easy to scan at presentation speed.

Final Circuit Scorecard

Each cell compares 2026 with 2025 at the same circuit. The color shows whether the number increased or decreased; the text gives the exact 2025 to 2026 change.

Methodology

The analysis uses FastF1 race-session data, then summarizes each race into a small set of presentation-friendly measures. D3.js renders the charts directly in the page so every view can be inspected with tooltips, while Scrollama helps coordinate the scroll-based transition moments between sections.

Data

FastF1 provided lap timing, position, speed-trap, and session status data for Australia, China, Japan, Miami, and Canada across 2025 and 2026.

Metrics

Green-flag lap pace, fastest lap, clean-lap speed trap, neutralized laps, DNFs, and a position-change proxy were derived for comparison.

Visualization

D3.js powers the bars, step charts, slope graph, timeline heatmap, scorecard, legends, and interactive tooltips.

Story Layer

Scrollama supports the arcade-style scrollytelling transitions, with Codex-assisted iteration used for implementation, assets, report assembly, and verification.
